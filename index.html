Q1 1 mark Listen to the following piece of music. If you were a member of the public and wanted to hear this music at home, what format would you have purchased it in?
a) 45 RPM record
b) 7-inch vinyl record
c) Sheet music
d) Cassette
Answer

c) Sheet music. The Maple Leaf Rag was published in 1899 — long before recorded formats existed. It sold over a million copies as sheet music.

Q2 1 mark Listen to the following song. Which subset of the blues genre best describes it?
a) Vaudeville (aka classic) Blues
b) English (aka white) Blues
c) Chicago (aka electric) Blues
d) Delta (aka folkloric) Blues
Answer

d) Delta (aka folkloric) Blues. Robert Johnson recorded in Mississippi in the 1930s. Solo acoustic guitar, minimal accompaniment, personal storytelling — hallmarks of the Delta tradition.

Q3 1 mark The word 'teenager' had existed for a long time but became commonplace in the 1950s. Who popularised this term?
a) Musicians looking for a new market
b) The advertising and marketing industry
c) Teenagers themselves
d) The president of the United States
Answer

b) The advertising and marketing industry. Advertisers identified teenagers as a new consumer group with disposable income and targeted them as a distinct market segment.

Q5 2 marks Where was 'the Brill Building' and what was it used for?
Answer

1619 Broadway, New York City. A hub for professional songwriting and music publishing — where songwriters created pop songs for artists to record and release.

Q10 1 mark What is 'Skiffle' music?
a) A mix of trad jazz, blues and folk that was popular in the U.K. and very influential on the Beatles and other British Invasion era bands.
b) A mix of Rhythm and Blues, Hillbilly and Big Band music that American soldiers listened to during World War 2.
c) Songs written by professional songwriters in Tin Pan Alley and sold to upcoming pop singers in the 1940s and 50s.
d) A style of music that emerged from Jamaica in the 1950s blending Caribbean and American rhythm and blues influences.
Answer

a) A mix of trad jazz, blues and folk popular in the UK. Key artist: Lonnie Donegan. Directly influenced the early Beatles and the broader British Invasion.

Q11 3 marks Comment on the Beatles' influence and impact on popular music. (3–4 sentences, ~80–100 words)
Key points (any 3–4)
  • Band format and self-written songs — changed how artists were expected to work
  • Instigated the British Invasion, opening the US market to UK acts
  • Studio experimentation — non-Western instruments, orchestral arrangements, experimental sound effects
  • Albums such as Revolver (1966) and Sgt. Pepper's (1967) treated pop as art
  • Influenced countless artists across every subsequent genre
  • Cultural phenomenon — Beatlemania changed what it meant to be a global pop star

Q17 5 marks Comment on how MTV revolutionised the way music was marketed and consumed in the 1980s. (3–4 sentences, ~80–100 words)
Key points
  • First TV network to exclusively play music videos, 24/7
  • Made the music video a must-have marketing tool for all artists
  • Artists could achieve commercial success through MTV without radio play
  • Initially excluded Black artists — Billie Jean by Michael Jackson helped break that barrier
  • Massive influence on fashion, hair, personal image and youth culture
  • Creativity became holistic — music and visual art combined
  • Key artists: Michael Jackson (Thriller), Madonna, Prince, Kate Bush, Dire Straits, David Bowie

Q20 5 marks Name the preferred music format for consuming music in each of the following decades.
1940s – 50s
1960s – 70s
1970s – 80s
1980s – 90s
1990s – today
Answer
  • 1940s–50s: 78 RPM records, 45 RPM singles, jukeboxes
  • 1960s–70s: LP vinyl records
  • 1970s–80s: LP vinyl, cassettes, 12-inch singles
  • 1980s–90s: Compact discs (CD), MTV
  • 1990s–today: MP3s, streaming services, mobile devices
